# Temperature Derangements, Sepsis, and Systemic Inflammatory Response
Source: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) Sepsis Clinical Guide / Surviving Sepsis Campaign
Authority: CDC / Society of Critical Care Medicine (SCCM)

## Body Temperature Extremes
Normal core body temperature is approximately 36.5°C to 37.5°C (97.7°F to 99.5°F).
- Severe Hypothermia (< 35.0°C): Impairs cardiac conduction, reduces platelet function, risks ventricular fibrillation, and is a hallmark of the "trauma lethal triad" (hypothermia, coagulopathy, acidosis).
- Fever / Pyrexia (>= 38.0°C): Standard physiological response to infection or severe inflammation.
- Hyperpyrexia (> 39.5°C): Markedly increases metabolic demand, cardiac workload, and risk of febrile delirium or seizure.

## Sepsis Identification in Emergency Triage
Sepsis is life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection.
Quick Sepsis-related Organ Failure Assessment (qSOFA) criteria include:
1. Respiratory rate >= 22 bpm
2. Altered mental status (GCS < 15)
3. Systolic blood pressure <= 100 mmHg

The presence of 2 or more qSOFA points indicates high risk of in-hospital mortality or prolonged ICU stay.

## Geriatric Vulnerability
Elderly patients (Age >= 65) frequently fail to mount a robust febrile response. In geriatric sepsis, severe infection often presents atypical symptoms: hypothermia, sudden confusion/delirium (altered GCS), or unexplained hypotension without overt high fever.
