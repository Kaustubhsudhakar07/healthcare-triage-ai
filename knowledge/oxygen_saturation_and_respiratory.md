# Pulse Oximetry (SpO2) and Respiratory Distress
Source: World Health Organization (WHO) Pulse Oximetry Manual / MedlinePlus (NIH)
Authority: WHO / American Thoracic Society

## Clinical Definition
Pulse oximetry (SpO2) measures the percentage of hemoglobin binding sites in the bloodstream occupied by oxygen. Normal adult resting arterial oxygen saturation at sea level is 95% to 100%.

## Clinical Thresholds & Acuity Bands
- Normal / Adequate Oxygenation: 95% - 100%
- Mild Hypoxemia: 91% - 94% (Requires supplemental nasal cannula O2 and close observation)
- Moderate Hypoxemia: 88% - 90% (Urgent clinical intervention, high-flow O2 therapy, investigation for pulmonary embolism, pneumonia, or acute pulmonary edema)
- Severe Hypoxemic Respiratory Failure: < 88% (Life-threatening emergency; risk of rapid tissue hypoxia, cardiac arrest, and cellular death; high-flow mask O2 or non-invasive/invasive mechanical ventilation required)

## Respiratory Rate (Tachypnea and Bradypnea)
- Normal Resting Adult Rate: 12 to 20 breaths per minute (bpm)
- Bradypnea (< 10 bpm): Indicates central nervous system depression, severe narcotic/sedative toxicity, or terminal respiratory exhaustion
- Tachypnea (> 24 bpm): Early compensatory physiological sign of hypoxemia, metabolic acidosis (septic shock), or pulmonary consolidation
- Severe Respiratory Distress (> 30 bpm): Pre-arrest indicator of respiratory muscle failure

## Synergy with Cardiovascular Signs
When severe hypoxemia (SpO2 < 90%) co-occurs with tachypnea (> 25 bpm) and tachycardia (HR > 110 bpm), patient acuity shifts exponentially into the Critical triage tier.
