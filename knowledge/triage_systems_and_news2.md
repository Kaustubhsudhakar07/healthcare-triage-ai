# Emergency Triage Methodologies and Early Warning Scores
Source: National Health Service (NHS) / Royal College of Physicians (NEWS2) / CDC START Triage Protocol
Authority: Royal College of Physicians UK / Emergency Nurses Association (ESI)

## Emergency Triage Frameworks
Triage is the process of rapidly determining patient medical urgency to ensure life-threatened individuals receive immediate resuscitation:

1. Emergency Severity Index (ESI):
   - Level 1: Immediate resuscitation required (unresponsive, apnea, pulseless)
   - Level 2: High risk, altered mental state, severe pain, or dangerous vital signs
   - Level 3: Stable, but requires two or more hospital resources
   - Level 4: Stable, requires one hospital resource
   - Level 5: Non-urgent, requires zero hospital resources

2. Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment (START) for Mass Casualties:
   - Immediate (Red): Compromised airway, respiratory rate > 30, absent radial pulse, or unable to follow commands
   - Delayed (Yellow): Serious injuries requiring medical care, but non-life-threatening within 1 hour
   - Minimal / Walking Wounded (Green): Able to walk and follow commands
   - Expectant / Deceased (Black): Unresponsive with non-viable trauma or no spontaneous breathing after airway opening

3. National Early Warning Score 2 (NEWS2):
   - Standardized 6-parameter scoring system tracking Respiration Rate, SpO2, Systolic BP, Heart Rate, Level of Consciousness (ACVPU), and Temperature.
   - Aggregate score >= 7 triggers emergency medical team response for critical illness or sepsis.

## 5-Tier Criticality Score Mapping in this System:
- Low [1.0 - 2.4]: Routine ambulatory intake; vitals stable; nursing review within 120 min.
- Moderate [2.5 - 4.4]: Acute care bay; evaluation and monitoring within 60 min.
- Elevated [4.5 - 6.4]: Monitored ED bay; physician assessment within 30 min.
- High [6.5 - 8.4]: High-acuity resuscitation bay; immediate physician evaluation and continuous telemetry.
- Critical [8.5 - 10.0]: Code 1 Trauma / Resuscitation bay; immediate attending physician, airway team, and aggressive stabilization.
