# Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) Clinical Reference Guide
Source: National Institutes of Health (NIH) / National Library of Medicine (StatPearls)
Authority: NIH / CDC Emergency Traumatic Brain Injury Guidelines

## Clinical Definition
The Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) is an objective clinical scale used to assess a patient's level of consciousness and neurological functioning following acute brain injury, trauma, intoxication, or severe metabolic derangement.

## Scoring Components
GCS is scored between 3 (deep coma or brain death) and 15 (fully conscious, alert, and oriented) across three behavioral response categories:

1. Eye Opening (E):
   - 4: Spontaneous eye opening
   - 3: To verbal command / speech
   - 2: To painful stimulus
   - 1: None

2. Verbal Response (V):
   - 5: Oriented and converses normally
   - 4: Disoriented and confused conversation
   - 3: Inappropriate words
   - 2: Incomprehensible sounds / groans
   - 1: None

3. Motor Response (M):
   - 6: Obeys commands
   - 5: Localizes to painful stimulus
   - 4: Flexion withdrawal to pain
   - 3: Decorticate abnormal posturing (flexion)
   - 2: Decerebrate abnormal posturing (extension)
   - 1: None (flaccid)

## Severity Classifications
- Severe Neurological Impairment: GCS 3 to 8 (Coma; universally mandates definitive airway management / endotracheal intubation)
- Moderate Brain Injury: GCS 9 to 12
- Mild Brain Injury / Alert: GCS 13 to 15

## Pre-Hospital Triage Implications
A GCS <= 8 is a critical red flag in emergency triage, indicating that the patient has lost protective airway reflexes, risking aspiration, hypoxemia, and immediate mortality.
