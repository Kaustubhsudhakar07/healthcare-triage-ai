# Hemodynamics, Blood Pressure and Shock Index
Source: National Institutes of Health (NIH) / National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI StatPearls)
Authority: American College of Surgeons Trauma Quality Improvement Program (TQIP) / European Society of Intensive Care Medicine

## Clinical Definition of Shock
Shock is a state of systemic hypoperfusion resulting in inadequate cellular oxygen delivery. If left untreated, cellular dysoxia leads to anaerobic metabolism, lactic acidosis, multiorgan dysfunction syndrome (MODS), and death.

## Shock Index (SI)
Shock Index is calculated as the ratio of Heart Rate (HR) to Systolic Blood Pressure (SBP):
$$\text{Shock Index} = \frac{\text{Heart Rate (bpm)}}{\text{Systolic BP (mmHg)}}$$

### Normal Range & Risk Strata:
- Normal: 0.5 to 0.7
- Mild Shock / Occult Hypoperfusion: 0.7 to 0.9
- Moderate Shock: 0.9 to 1.1 (High suspicion of early compensated hemorrhage or sepsis)
- Severe / Decompensated Shock: > 1.1 (Significant increase in mortality, immediate transfusion requirement, or ICU transfer)

### Why Shock Index Matters in Pre-Hospital Care:
In hemorrhagic and septic shock, compensatory vasoconstriction can maintain a normal Systolic BP while the heart rate increases. Thus, hypotension is a *late* sign of decompensation. Shock Index reveals occult hypoperfusion before overt blood pressure collapse.

## Blood Pressure Strata
- Normal Adult SBP: 100 to 120 mmHg
- Elevated / Hypertensive Urgency: SBP > 180 mmHg or DBP > 120 mmHg
- Borderline Hypotension: SBP 85 to 95 mmHg
- Severe Hypotensive Shock: SBP < 85 mmHg (Life-threatening loss of cerebral and coronary perfusion pressure)

## Pulse Pressure (PP)
Pulse pressure is defined as Systolic BP minus Diastolic BP ($PP = SBP - DBP$).
- Narrow Pulse Pressure (< 30 mmHg): Suggests hypovolemic shock, cardiac tamponade, or severe cardiogenic shock.
- Wide Pulse Pressure (> 60 mmHg): Suggests hyperdynamic septic shock, aortic regurgitation, or severe arterial stiffness.
